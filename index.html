Family & green cards

U.S. citizens and permanent residents can sponsor spouses, children, parents, and siblings. We build the petition, gather the evidence, and prepare you for the interview.

Who it is for

  • Spouses and fiancé(e)s of U.S. citizens
  • Parents, children, and siblings
  • Green card holders removing conditions

How we help

  • Choosing adjustment or consular processing
  • Affidavit of support and evidence review
  • Marriage interview preparation

Citizenship

Naturalization is the last step for many of our clients. We check your eligibility, flag anything in your history that needs attention, and get you ready for the civics test and interview.

Who it is for

  • Green card holders of 3 or 5 years
  • Children who may already be citizens
  • Applicants with past arrests or long trips abroad

How we help

  • Eligibility and travel-history review
  • Application and fee waiver requests
  • Interview and civics test preparation

Deportation defense

If you or a family member has received a Notice to Appear or has a hearing in immigration court, time matters. We review your options and represent you before the judge.

Who it is for

  • People in removal proceedings
  • Family members of someone detained
  • Anyone with an old removal order

How we help

  • Bond hearings
  • Cancellation of removal and other relief
  • Motions to reopen and appeals

Asylum

For people who fear returning to their home country. Deadlines are strict, and a well-documented application makes a real difference. We help you tell your story clearly and support it with evidence.

Who it is for

  • People who fear persecution at home
  • Applicants near the one-year filing deadline
  • Families applying together

How we help

  • Declaration and country-conditions evidence
  • Affirmative and defensive applications
  • Work permit while the case is pending
Ask about asylum →

DACA & TPS

Renewals have to be on time, and the rules change often. We track the deadlines, file renewals, and look at whether a more permanent option is open to you.

Who it is for

  • Current DACA recipients
  • Nationals of TPS-designated countries
  • Anyone unsure whether they still qualify

How we help

  • Renewals and work permits
  • Travel permission (advance parole)
  • Screening for green card options
Ask about a renewal →

Waivers

Past unlawful presence, a prior visa problem, or an old conviction does not always end a case. A waiver can forgive certain problems when your family would face hardship.

Who it is for

  • Spouses and children of citizens with past overstays
  • Applicants with prior immigration violations
  • People with certain criminal records

How we help

  • Assessing whether a waiver is available
  • Building the hardship case
  • Coordinating with consular processing

Before your consultation

What to bring.

You do not need everything on this list. Bring what you have and we will tell you what is missing.

  1. Passport and visasCurrent and expired, including any visa stamps
  2. Your I-94 recordYour most recent entry record, printable from the CBP website
  3. Notices from USCISReceipt and approval notices (I-797) or any letters you received
  4. Family documentsBirth and marriage certificates, divorce decrees, with translations
  5. Court or police recordsAny arrests, tickets, or court papers, even old or dismissed ones
  6. Travel historyApproximate dates you entered and left the U.S.

Questions

Answers to common questions.

Do I have to come to the office?

No. You can meet with us at 136 Madison Avenue, or by phone or video from anywhere. Immigration law is federal, so we can help clients across the United States.

How long will my case take?

It depends on the type of case and the office handling it. USCIS publishes current processing times on its website, and we will give you a realistic estimate for your situation at your consultation.

I have a court date. Is it too late to hire a lawyer?

No. Contact us as soon as possible and bring your Notice to Appear and any papers from the court. Never miss a hearing, even if you do not have a lawyer yet.

Is what I tell you confidential?

Conversations with your attorney for the purpose of getting legal advice are protected. Please do not send sensitive details through the website form; we will discuss them privately at your consultation.

What languages do you speak?

We work with clients in English, Russian (Русский), and Kazakh (Қазақша). If you speak another language, tell us when you book and we will arrange an interpreter.
